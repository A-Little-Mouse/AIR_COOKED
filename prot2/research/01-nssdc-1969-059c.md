# NSSDC 1969-059C — Apollo 11 Lunar Module / EASEP

## Identity and mission

The specified NSSDC record identifies **1969-059C** as **Apollo 11 Lunar Module / EASEP**. The Lunar Module was named **Eagle**; the record also lists LM-5 and Apollo 11 LM/EASEP among its alternate names. [1]

- **Launch date:** 1969-07-16. The launch vehicle was Saturn 5, from Cape Canaveral, United States. [1]
- **Agency/program:** NASA — Office of Manned Space Flight (United States); Apollo 11 lunar-landing mission. The NSSDC record lists Planetary Science and Human Crew as the disciplines. [1]
- **Mission type:** Crewed lunar landing and lunar-surface scientific operations, followed by ascent-stage rendezvous and docking with the Apollo 11 Command/Service Module. This classification is a direct synthesis of the NSSDC mission description, not a separate NSSDC field. [1]
- **Objective:** Land the first crewed vehicle on the Moon, support Armstrong and Aldrin during lunar-surface operations, collect lunar samples, and deploy the Early Apollo Scientific Experiment Package (EASEP) and other instruments. EASEP was intended to return measurements of seismic activity and lunar-crust/interior properties and to measure dust accumulation and radiation damage to solar cells. [1]

## Key achievements

- Eagle made the first crewed lunar landing in Mare Tranquillitatis (the Sea of Tranquility) on 20 July 1969 at 20:17:40 UT. [1]
- Neil A. Armstrong and Edwin E. “Buzz” Aldrin became the first people to walk on the Moon; Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit in the CSM. [1]
- The astronauts collected **21.55 kg** of lunar rock and soil, took two core-tube samples, photographed the site, and deployed the EASEP and a laser-ranging retroreflector. [1]
- The astronauts traversed about **250 m** in total, with both ranging up to about 100 m from the Lunar Module. [1]
- EASEP operated despite temperatures exceeding its planned maximum by 30 °C. It transmitted until ground controllers stopped it near the first lunar sunset, was switched on again on the next lunar day, and was terminated on 27 August 1969 after it stopped accepting commands. [1]

## Key specifications available in the NSSDC record

- **Total LM mass:** 15,103 kg, including ascent and descent stages and propellants. [1]
- **Ascent-stage dry mass:** 2,445 kg; ascent-stage propellant: 2,376 kg. [1]
- **Descent-stage dry mass:** 2,034 kg, including stowed surface equipment; initial descent-stage propellant: 8,248 kg. [1]
- **Descent stage:** Octagonal prism, 4.2 m across and 1.7 m thick; landing legs placed the stage bottom 1.5 m above the surface; opposite-footpad span 9.4 m. [1]
- **Ascent stage:** Approximately 2.8 m high and 4.0 by 4.3 m wide; pressurized crew-compartment volume 6.65 m³. [1]
- **Propulsion:** Deep-throttling descent engine, maximum thrust about 45,000 N; fixed constant-thrust ascent engine, about 15,000 N; four reaction-control thrust modules, each with four 450 N thrust chambers. [1]
- **Power and guidance:** Six silver-zinc batteries; radar ranging, inertial measurement unit with gyroscopes and accelerometers, and the Apollo Guidance Computer. [1]
- **EASEP:** Total mass 48 kg; uplink 2119 MHz and telemetry downlink 2276.5 MHz. It included two solar panels, communications equipment, a passive seismometer, a lunar dust detector, an isotope heater, antenna mast, and associated base hardware. [1]
- **Landing site:** 0.67416° N, 23.47314° E in Mare Tranquillitatis, using the reference frame and coordinates stated by NSSDC. [1]

## Status

The ascent stage lifted off the Moon on 21 July 1969 at 17:54:01 UT, rendezvoused and docked with the CSM, and the LM was jettisoned into lunar orbit at 00:01:01 UT on 22 July. NSSDC states that the LM’s subsequent fate is unknown, although it is assumed to have crashed into the lunar surface within the following one to four months. The EASEP experiment was terminated on 27 August 1969 after it stopped accepting commands. [1]

## Interesting facts

1. The landing site is now commonly known as Tranquility Base; the NSSDC record preserves the exact landing time and coordinates and the famous first transmission, “Houston, Tranquility Base here — the Eagle has landed.” [1]
2. The LM had no seats. Its crew compartment was a pressurized operating cabin, and the astronauts controlled the vehicle from a console and side panels. [1]
3. The Apollo 11 EASEP was the predecessor to the ALSEP packages used on later Apollo missions, but it was designed to operate only during lunar daylight because it relied on solar panels. [1]
