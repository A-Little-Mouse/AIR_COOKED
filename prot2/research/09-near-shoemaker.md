# NEAR Shoemaker

## Mission identification

NASA’s official mission page identifies the spacecraft as **NEAR Shoemaker**. NEAR is the acronym for **Near Earth Asteroid Rendezvous**; NASA renamed the spacecraft NEAR Shoemaker on March 14, 2000, in honor of geologist Eugene M. Shoemaker. [1]

- **Launch date:** February 17, 1996; NASA gives the launch time as 20:43:27 UT. [1]
- **Agency/program:** NASA, the first mission in NASA’s Discovery Program. Mission design and management were NASA / Goddard Space Flight Center / Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (APL); APL designed and built the spacecraft and managed the mission for NASA. [1]
- **Mission type:** Flyby, orbiter, and lander. [1]
- **Target:** Asteroid 433 Eros, with a bonus flyby of asteroid 253 Mathilde. [1]
- **Objective:** Fly by Mathilde, rendezvous with and orbit Eros, and land on Eros. The primary science objective was to measure Eros’s physical properties, mineral composition, morphology, internal mass distribution, and magnetic field. [1]

## Key achievements

- NEAR became the first human-made object to orbit an asteroid, entering orbit around Eros on February 14, 2000. [1]
- It became the first spacecraft to land on an asteroid when it touched down on Eros on February 12, 2001. NASA also describes this as the first time a U.S. spacecraft was first to land on a celestial body. [1]
- During the June 27, 1997, Mathilde encounter, NEAR photographed about 60% of the asteroid from a closest range of about 1,200 km. [1]
- NEAR completed its Eros science goals and returned about 10 times more data than originally planned, including approximately 160,000 images. It mapped more than 70% of Eros’s surface using its near-infrared spectrometer and found that Eros had no magnetic field. [1]
- The spacecraft survived a landing it was not designed to perform and continued returning useful data, especially gamma-ray measurements, for about two weeks after touchdown. [1]

## Key specifications

- **Spacecraft mass:** 1,775 lb (805 kg). [1]
- **Launch vehicle:** Delta 7925-8, vehicle no. D232. [1]
- **Launch site:** Cape Canaveral, Florida, Launch Complex 17B. [1]
- **Scientific instruments:** Multi-Spectral Imager (MSI); Magnetometer (MAG); Near Infrared Spectrometer (NIS); X-Ray/Gamma-Ray Spectrometer (XGRS); Near-Earth Asteroid Rendezvous Laser Rangefinder (NLR); and a radio-science and gravimetry experiment. [1]
- **Eros operational orbit:** NASA reports an early orbital geometry of approximately 200 × 225 miles (321 × 366 km), later reduced to an operational altitude of about 31 miles (50 km) from Eros’s center and then to a circular low orbit of about 22 miles (35 km). [1]
- **Touchdown:** February 12, 2001, at 19:44 UT, at about 4 mph (6.4 km/h), just south of the saddle-shaped Himeros feature. [1]

## Status

The mission is complete. NEAR’s last contact was February 28, 2001, after the spacecraft succumbed to extreme cold of approximately −279°F (−173°C). NASA’s later attempt to contact it on December 10, 2002, was unsuccessful. [1]

## Interesting facts

1. NEAR was the first spacecraft to rely on solar cells for power during operations beyond Mars orbit. [1]
2. A December 20, 1998, engine-burn problem forced a revised trajectory. The spacecraft consequently made an unplanned first flyby of Eros on December 23, 1998, before entering orbit more than a year later. [1]
3. NEAR was not designed as a lander, yet it survived its gentle Eros touchdown and operated from the surface for roughly two weeks. [1] NASA/JPL reported that the final descent covered a total mission journey of about 3.2 billion km (2 billion miles). [2]
