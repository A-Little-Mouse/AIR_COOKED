# Viking 1

**Source scope.** Mission facts in this report were extracted from and verified against NASA’s official Viking 1 mission page [1]. The separate model section uses NASA’s official 3D Resources catalog, NASA’s official 3D-Resources GitHub repository, NASA’s media-usage guidance, and NASA’s data catalog because the request specifically asks for an official downloadable model and its technical/licensing metadata.

## Mission profile

- **Official name:** Viking 1 [1]
- **Launch date:** August 20, 1975 [1]
- **Agency/program:** NASA / Langley Research Center (LaRC) / Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) [1]
- **Mission type:** Orbiter and lander [1]
- **Objective:** Mars landing and orbit [1]

Viking 1 was designed to place an orbiter around Mars and safely land a scientific spacecraft on the surface. The lander investigated the local environment and soil, including experiments for signs of life and organic compounds, while the orbiter imaged and mapped Mars. [1]

## Key achievements

- Viking 1 made the first truly successful landing on Mars. [1]
- Its lander returned high-quality surface photographs, including a 300-degree panorama, and scooped Martian soil for biological and chemical experiments. The experiments found abundant sulfur and negative results for organic compounds. [1]
- The orbiter returned 52,663 images, mapped about 97% of Mars’s surface, and obtained high-resolution views of Mars and its moons. [1]
- The lander operated until 1982 and the orbiter until 1980, extending the mission well beyond its primary phase and supplying a substantial baseline for later Mars exploration. [1]

## Key specifications reported by NASA

- **Spacecraft mass:** 7,776 lb (3,527 kg) [1]
- **Power:** The lander used two SNAP-19 radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs); the orbiter was solar-powered. [1]
- **Instruments:** Imaging systems, infrared spectrometers, a seismometer (inoperable), a gas chromatograph–mass spectrometer, and weather instruments, among others. [1]
- **Mission elements:** One orbiter and one lander. [1]

## Status

The Viking 1 mission is complete. The lander’s communications ended on November 11, 1982, after a faulty command. The orbiter was shut down on August 7, 1980, after it ran out of attitude-control propellant. [1]

## Interesting facts

1. Viking 1 landed on July 20, 1976, and became the first spacecraft to achieve a truly successful landing on Mars. [1]
2. The lander’s cameras produced a 300-degree panorama of its surroundings, while the orbiter’s imaging campaign covered about 97% of the Martian surface. [1]
3. Viking’s biology experiments were an early direct attempt to test Martian soil for life-related activity; NASA’s mission summary records negative results for organic compounds despite abundant sulfur. [1]

## Official 3D model search

A suitable official model is available for the **Viking lander**, which is one of the two spacecraft elements of Viking 1. NASA’s 3D Resources page identifies the source as “NASA/Michael D. Carbajal,” links the model to the Viking 1 mission, and provides a GLB download. [2] The official NASA 3D-Resources GitHub directory contains the matching filename **`Viking Lander.glb`** and a preview image. [3]

### Model candidate

- **File name:** `Viking Lander.glb`
- **Source URL:** https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/viking-lander/
- **Direct download URL:** https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/3d/resources/model/viking-lander/Viking%20Lander.glb?emrc=6af7eb6375d2c
- **Format:** GLB, glTF Binary 2.0. A direct download was verified locally as a valid glTF binary model, version 2.
- **File size:** NASA’s model page lists **1.76 MB** [2]. A direct download measured **1,849,384 bytes** (about 1.85 MB in decimal units, or 1.76 MiB).
- **Polygon count:** **Not stated for this GLB on the NASA 3D Resources page or in the linked GitHub directory.** NASA’s separate legacy data catalog record for “NASA 3D Models: Viking Lander” reports **99,526 polygons and 50,502 vertices**, but it points to an older `viking-c.zip` 3DS resource rather than explicitly identifying the newer GLB. Therefore those counts are not assigned to the GLB. [5]
- **License terms:** The NASA 3D-Resources repository README says its assets are “free and without copyright” and points users to NASA usage guidelines. [3] NASA’s current media guidance says content used in the rendition of 3D models, including texture maps and polygon data, is generally not subject to U.S. copyright and may be used for educational or informational purposes, with NASA acknowledged as the source. The guidance also warns that NASA identifiers and logos are protected, NASA endorsement must not be implied, and third-party copyrighted material may require separate permission. [4] The NASA data catalog record itself says “License not specified,” so this should be treated as NASA-use guidance rather than a conventional SPDX license grant. [5]
- **Confidence:** High that the file is an official NASA Viking lander model and that the download URL and filename are correct; medium on unrestricted reuse because the catalog record does not specify a formal license and NASA’s guidance contains attribution, endorsement, logo, and possible third-party-content conditions.

**Model status:** `available_with_caveat`. An official downloadable NASA model exists for the Viking lander, not a clearly identified complete orbiter-plus-lander assembly.
