# NASA Surveyor 3

## Mission identity and outcome

**Official name:** Surveyor 3. NASA’s mission page identifies the spacecraft in its mission table as **Surveyor-C**. [1]

**Launch date:** April 17, 1967, at 07:05:01 UT. [1]

**Agency/program:** NASA / Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), under the Surveyor lunar soft-landing program. The NASA page lists “Mission Design and Management” as NASA / JPL. [1]

**Mission type:** Lunar lander; the NASA page labels its type “Lander.” [1]

**Objective:** Make a soft landing on the Moon. The mission table describes the objective as “Lunar Soft-Landing.” [1]

**Status:** Inactive. Surveyor 3 landed on April 20, 1967, and its last contact was May 4, 1967, two days after lunar night began. Apollo 12 astronauts later visited the inactive lander on November 19, 1969, and recovered parts including the soil scoop and camera system. [1]

## Key achievements

- Surveyor 3 was the second Surveyor spacecraft to make a soft landing on the Moon. [1]
- Its surface-sampling experiments supported the conclusion that lunar soil was solid enough to support an Apollo Lunar Module. [1]
- The lander transmitted the first of 6,326 television pictures less than an hour after landing. [1]
- The surface sampler dug four trenches, performed four bearing tests, and conducted thirteen impact tests. [1]
- Apollo 12 recovered Surveyor 3 components after nearly two-and-a-half years of exposure on the lunar surface, providing material for assessing the effects of the lunar environment. [1]

## Key specifications and mission data

- **Spacecraft mass:** 2,200 pounds (997.9 kilograms). [1]
- **Launch vehicle:** Atlas Centaur (AC-12 / Atlas D No. 292 / Centaur D). [1]
- **Launch site:** Cape Canaveral, Florida, Launch Complex 36B. [1]
- **Target and landing region:** Moon; southeastern Oceanus Procellarum, at 3° south latitude and 23.41° west longitude. [1]
- **Scientific instruments:** TV camera and surface sampler. [1]
- **Surface sampler:** Could reach up to 5 feet (1.5 meters) from the lander and dig up to 7 inches (18 centimeters) deep. [1]
- **Sampler operations:** Four trenches, four bearing tests, and thirteen impact tests. [1]
- **Television camera:** Two lenses with 25 mm and 100 mm focal lengths; approximately 20 seconds to transmit a single 200-line picture; capable of 600-line digital picture transmission. [1]
- **Lunar-soil result:** The experiments indicated a wet-sand-like consistency and a bearing strength of about 10 pounds per square inch (0.7 kilograms per square centimeter, or 98 kilopascals). [1]

## Interesting facts

1. Surveyor 3 bounced twice during descent because reflective rocks confused its descent radar and the main engine did not cut off correctly at about 14 feet (4.3 meters) altitude. The first bounce reached 33 feet (10 meters), the second 10 feet (3 meters), and the third contact became the soft landing. [1]
2. The spacecraft’s TV camera was mounted beneath a movable mirror, allowing horizontal and vertical pointing; it had both 200-line and 600-line imaging modes. [1]
3. Apollo 12’s Intrepid Lunar Module landed approximately 197 yards (180 meters) from Surveyor 3, and astronauts Charles Conrad Jr. and Alan L. Bean walked to the inactive probe during their second EVA. [1]
