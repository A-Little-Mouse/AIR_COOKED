# NASA Mars Pathfinder — verified mission and 3D-model research

## Mission identity

- **Official name:** Mars Pathfinder. The lander was named the **Carl Sagan Memorial Station** and its small rover was **Sojourner**. [1]
- **Launch date:** December 4, 1996 UTC. [1]
- **Agency/program:** NASA; Mars Pathfinder was the second mission in NASA’s **Discovery Program**, a program of low-cost spacecraft with focused science goals. [1] [2]
- **Mission type:** Lander and rover. [1]
- **Primary objective:** Land a rover on Mars. More specifically, the mission was designed as a technology demonstration to deliver an instrumented lander and a free-ranging robotic rover to the Martian surface, while collecting atmospheric, geological, and engineering data. [1]

## Objective and mission design

Pathfinder demonstrated a new direct-entry landing approach for Mars. A parachute slowed the descent, then a large airbag system cushioned impact. The lander carried instruments for atmospheric, climate, geology, and rock-and-soil investigations; Sojourner carried instruments for surface observations and technology/engineering measurements. [1]

The landing site was Ares Vallis, an ancient flood plain selected because it was considered relatively safe and offered a broad variety of rocks deposited by catastrophic flooding. [1]

## Key achievements

- Successfully landed on Mars on July 4, 1997 and deployed the first-ever robotic rover delivered to the Martian surface, Sojourner. [1]
- Demonstrated the first use of the airbag landing technique on Mars. Pathfinder bounced about 15 times before coming to rest. [1]
- Returned **2.3 billion bits** of information, including more than **16,500 lander images**, **550 rover images**, more than **15 chemical analyses** of rocks and soil, and extensive wind and weather data. [1]
- Produced science results consistent with a warmer, wetter ancient Mars. Rounded pebbles and cobbles suggested conglomerates formed by running water; observations also documented dust devils, early-morning water-ice clouds, magnetic airborne dust, and strong morning temperature changes. [1]
- Radio tracking precisely measured the lander’s location and Mars’ pole of rotation, constraining the radius of Mars’ metallic core to greater than about 800 miles (1,300 km) and less than roughly 1,250 miles (2,000 km). [1]

## Key specifications and dates

- **Launch vehicle:** Delta II 7925. [1]
- **Launch location:** Cape Canaveral Air Force Station, Florida. [1]
- **Mars landing date:** July 4, 1997. [1]
- **Landing site:** Ares Vallis, Mars. [1]
- **Sojourner mass:** 23 lb (10.6 kg). [1]
- **Sojourner height when deployed:** 1 ft (30 cm). [1]
- **Landing system:** parachute plus airbags; the airbag-encased spacecraft was about 19 ft (5.8 m) in diameter during the bounce sequence and reached roughly 31 mph (14 m/s) at impact. [1]
- **Mission end listed by NASA:** September 27, 1997. [1]
- **Instruments:** Alpha Proton X-ray Spectrometer; three cameras; and the Atmospheric Structure Instrument/Meteorology Package. [1]

## Status

**Completed / concluded.** NASA’s mission page lists the end of mission as September 27, 1997. The same official page says the final data transmission occurred on that date. [1] The currently relevant status is therefore an historical, completed mission rather than an active spacecraft operation.

## Interesting facts

1. Pathfinder’s airbag-protected spacecraft bounced approximately 15 times, as high as about 50 ft (15 m), and rolled for roughly 2.5 minutes before stopping about 0.6 mile (1 km) from the initial impact point. [1]
2. The lander’s memorial name honored astronomer Carl Sagan, while the rover’s name honored civil-rights crusader Sojourner Truth. [1]
3. Sojourner descended a rear ramp tilted 20 degrees and stood up to full height on the second Martian day (sol 2), before beginning surface operations. [1]
