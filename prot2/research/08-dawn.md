# NASA Dawn mission research

## Mission record

**Official name:** Dawn. The specified NASA mission page identifies the spacecraft and mission simply as “Dawn.” [1]

**Launch date:** September 27, 2007. [1]

**Agency/program:** NASA mission; the NASA page’s mission simulation is credited to NASA/JPL-Caltech. The page does not name a separate program beyond NASA’s Dawn mission entry. [1]

**Mission type:** Orbiter. [1]

**Objective:** The mission’s stated operational objective on the specified NASA page is represented by its extended stays at two different small bodies: giant asteroid Vesta and dwarf planet Ceres. Both are in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. In other words, Dawn was designed to orbit and investigate Vesta and Ceres rather than conduct a flyby of only one target. [1]

**Key achievements:**

- NASA describes Dawn as “NASA’s first truly interplanetary spaceship.” [1]
- It completed extended stays at both Vesta and Ceres, two very different bodies in the main asteroid belt. [1]
- The NASA page lists its targets as Vesta and Ceres and its overall status as “Successful.” [1]

**Key specifications available from the specified page:**

- Spacecraft/mission type: Orbiter. [1]
- Launch date: Sept. 27, 2007. [1]
- Targets: Vesta and Ceres. [1]
- Location of targets: the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. [1]
- Mission-page status: Successful. [1]

The specified page does not provide spacecraft mass, dimensions, power, propulsion details, instrument list, orbital dates, or communications specifications. Those fields are therefore not added here as if they were verified by the requested source.

**Status:** Successful, as displayed on NASA’s mission page. [1]

## Interesting facts

1. Dawn was NASA’s first spacecraft characterized by NASA as “truly interplanetary.” [1]
2. It spent extended periods at two distinct extraterrestrial bodies rather than visiting only one target. [1]
3. Its two targets—Vesta and Ceres—are both small worlds in the asteroid belt, but one is described as a giant asteroid and the other as a dwarf planet. [1]

## Downloadable 3D model research

A suitable current official asset is available through NASA’s maintained `NASA-3D-Resources` GitHub mirror. The repository README says its models and related assets are “free and without copyright,” while NASA’s 3D Resources hub says the assets are free to download and use and directs users to NASA’s image and media usage guidelines. [2] [3] NASA’s guidance says NASA content used in the rendition of 3D models—including polygon data in any format—is generally not subject to U.S. copyright, may be used for educational or informational purposes, and should acknowledge NASA as the source. It also warns that NASA identifiers and logos are protected and that third-party material may have separate rights. [4]

### Primary verified model candidate

- **Exact file name:** `Dawn.glb`
- **Official source page:** https://github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources/blob/master/3D%20Models/Dawn/Dawn.glb
- **Direct download:** https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources/master/3D%20Models/Dawn/Dawn.glb
- **Format:** GLB (binary glTF 2.0)
- **License/terms:** NASA repository README: assets are free and without copyright; NASA’s linked media guidance applies. Use NASA attribution, do not imply NASA endorsement, and treat any separately identified third-party material as separately rights-managed. [2] [4]
- **Polygon count:** 32,096 triangles, verified by parsing the downloaded GLB’s glTF mesh primitives and summing indexed triangle primitives. The NASA page itself does not publish this count.
- **File size:** 927,020 bytes (verified from the direct download).
- **Confidence:** High. The file path is present in NASA’s official `nasa/NASA-3D-Resources` repository, and the raw file was downloaded and parsed successfully.

### Related NASA-published STL listing

NASA’s data catalog also records a Dawn STL resource named `Dawn_19.stl` and describes it as an STL made by scaling the original model and converting it directly to STL. The catalog gives the legacy direct resource URL as `http://nasa3d.arc.nasa.gov/shared_assets/models/jpl-vtad-dawn/Dawn_19.stl`, but that endpoint currently redirects to the NASA 3D Resources hub instead of serving the STL file. Because a stable direct download could not be verified, the STL is not treated as the primary downloadable candidate. The catalog also says “License not specified.” [5]

For context only, NASA’s official Sketchfab account has a “Dawn (3D printable)” page that reports an STL with 32.1k triangles and 16.4k vertices, credits Brian Kumanchik and Christian Lopez at NASA/JPL-Caltech, and states a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial license. The page does not expose a stable direct-download URL in its public extracted metadata, so it is not used as the primary direct-download record. [6]
